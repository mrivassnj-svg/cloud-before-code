Cloud Before Code — Lesson 6: Who Can Change the Menu?
Stage 1 of 6 · Setup
0:00 – 0:30
Something went wrong at LunchRush last night.
"LunchRush is a small food-ordering business. Last night, a customer was charged the wrong price — someone changed it at 11 p.m. No one knows who did it.

Jordan, the owner, asks: 'Does everyone on the team have access to everything?'

Before I teach you any vocabulary — think about this for 30 seconds."
Participants do: Listen. Prepare to respond.
Stage 2 of 6 · Exploratory Task
0:30 – 1:15
How does an organization decide who gets access to what?
"Think of your workplace, your school, or any organization you've been part of.

Write one sentence: What principle or question would you use to decide what access a person should have?

30 seconds. Go."
Participants do: Write one sentence on the handout. 1–2 volunteers share aloud.
"What you're describing — giving people what they need and not more — is a real security principle. There's a name for it. I'll tell you in a moment."
Stage 3 of 6 · Vocabulary Reveal
1:15 – 2:15
Three terms. Two minutes.
Authentication
Verifying who someone is. When you log in with a password, the system is authenticating you.
"Are you who you say you are?"
Authorization
Deciding what an authenticated user is allowed to do. Happens after authentication.
"What are you allowed to do?"
Least Privilege
Give each person only the access they need to do their job — nothing more.
That's the principle you just described.
"These two are often confused. Authentication is 'Who are you?' Authorization is 'What can you do?'

And the principle you described — giving people only what they need — that's called least privilege."

"Quick check — True or false: The cloud provider is responsible for making sure Sam can't change menu prices.

Raise your hand for True… raise your hand for False…"
FALSE.
Under shared responsibility, the cloud provider secures the physical infrastructure and the platform. The access policies we just built? Those are LunchRush's responsibility — not the provider's. That distinction matters a lot in real cloud security.
Stage 6 of 6 · Closing
4:15 – 5:00
What just happened — and why it's designed that way.
"Notice the sequence. I didn't start with 'today we're learning IAM.' I started with a business problem — a price that got changed. You articulated a solution in plain language before I introduced any vocabulary. Then you applied it immediately in a realistic activity.

That's the core design: explain it before you name it, then explain it twice — once in plain language, once using the right vocabulary.

I'm not claiming this design is proven. What I can say is it's deliberate, it's consistent across all eight lessons, and it runs on nothing more than a printed worksheet."
Authentication
Verifying identity — "Who are you?"
Authorization
Deciding permitted actions — "What can you do?"
Least Privilege
Minimum access needed. Nothing more.
