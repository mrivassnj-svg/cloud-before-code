### What I Plan to Create

I plan to design and produce a beginner-friendly, scenario-based curriculum that teaches introductory cloud-computing vocabulary through one continuous fictional business story. The project will be called **Cloud Before Code**. Learners will follow LunchRush, a small food-ordering and delivery business, as it moves from a local operation to a cloud-connected service. Each of the eight planned lessons will begin with a business challenge described in plain language. Only after learners have attempted to describe a solution in everyday terms will the technical vocabulary be introduced. This sequence is designed to reduce the barrier that unfamiliar jargon creates for people with no prior technology background.

### Who It Serves

Cloud Before Code is designed for students and nontechnical adults who have no prior cloud-computing experience. The curriculum assumes no programming knowledge, no networking background, and no access to paid software or cloud accounts. All core activities will work on paper or in editable text documents. The language will be direct, accessible, and appropriate for adult learners who may feel intimidated by technical subjects.

### How It Works

Each lesson will follow a consistent six-step teaching sequence: (1) encounter a business problem, (2) attempt a plain-language solution, (3) discover the cloud terminology, (4) complete a hands-on activity, (5) explain the solution in both plain and technical language, and (6) complete a brief understanding check. The curriculum uses three distinctive design features throughout. First, terminology is always introduced after the learner has worked through the problem in plain language. Second, each lesson includes an "explain it twice" task, in which learners practice explaining the concept without jargon to the business owner, Jordan, and with correct vocabulary to the technical helper, Alex. Third, learners will maintain a jargon journal with five-part entries for selected terms, including an explicit statement of where each analogy stops being accurate.

### How It Connects to Introductory Cloud Topics

The eight lessons will address foundational cloud concepts organized around realistic business problems: online presence and infrastructure, storage, compute options, networking and APIs, scaling, identity and access management, software delivery pipelines, and monitoring. These topics align with introductory-level cloud literacy frameworks and certification preparation areas, including cloud concepts, security, networking, and core services. All definitions will be vendor-neutral where possible, with provider-specific examples clearly labeled.

### What I Will Submit

The final submission will include: a project overview document with measurable learning objectives; an eight-lesson curriculum map; a fully developed instructor guide for three lessons with concise outlines for the remaining five; a learner workbook for the three fully developed lessons containing no answer keys; an assessment package with a pre-test, post-test, answer keys, scoring instructions, and a feedback survey; a pilot evaluation plan designed for three to five volunteers; a 10-slide class presentation with speaker notes; a five-minute live demonstration script; this proposal; a sources-and-verification document; and a production checklist. All files will be written in Markdown and stored in a structured project folder.

### How I Plan to Evaluate It

After completing the materials, I plan to recruit three to five volunteers who have no prior cloud-computing experience. I will administer the pre-test before the lessons begin and the post-test immediately after. I will collect the learner feedback survey and record facilitator observations during the session. I will compare pre-test and post-test scores to identify which concepts showed the greatest change and which did not. I will use the feedback survey and my observation notes to identify confusing instructions, unclear vocabulary, or timing issues. I will document these findings in the pilot results template and feedback summary template included in the project.

I will acknowledge that a pilot of three to five volunteers provides preliminary, qualitative feedback from a small convenience sample. The results will be intended to identify opportunities for revision, not to prove the effectiveness of the curriculum or to draw conclusions that apply broadly.

### What Is Outside the Scope

This project will not include a working web application, mobile application, or chatbot. It will not include live cloud infrastructure, code that learners must write or run, or accounts with any cloud provider or paid service. The pilot evaluation will not include completed scores, participant quotes, or summary findings, because the pilot has not yet occurred. The curriculum does not cover advanced cloud topics such as infrastructure as code, cloud networking architecture, container orchestration in depth, or cloud cost optimization. Fully developed lesson packs for Lessons 1, 4, 5, 7, and 8 are outside the scope of the initial submission; those lessons are provided as concise teaching outlines.
