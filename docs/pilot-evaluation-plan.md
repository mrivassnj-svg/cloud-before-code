# Pilot Evaluation Plan
## Cloud Before Code — LunchRush Curriculum

> **Purpose of this document:** This plan describes how the creator of Cloud Before Code can gather preliminary feedback from a small group of volunteers before submitting the project. All results tables and templates in this document are blank. Do not fill them with invented data, scores, or quotes. Actual findings require real participants.

---

## Purpose of the Pilot

The pilot is designed to:

1. Check whether learners can complete the activities as written without technical assistance.
2. Identify confusing instructions, unclear vocabulary, or timing issues.
3. Gather preliminary evidence of learning by comparing pre-test and post-test scores.
4. Collect learner and facilitator impressions through the feedback survey.

**What the pilot is not:** A small pilot with three to five volunteers does not establish the broad effectiveness of this curriculum, demonstrate statistical significance, or prove causation between instruction and learning outcomes. It provides preliminary, formative feedback to improve the materials before wider use.

---

## Participant Criteria

Invite volunteers who:

- Are 18 years of age or older.
- Have no prior formal training in cloud computing.
- Are willing to attend one session of approximately 60–75 minutes.
- Can complete written worksheets (or access a plain-text editable document).

**Number of participants:** Three to five volunteers.

**What to avoid:** Do not recruit participants who have worked professionally in IT, software development, or cloud administration — they would not represent the target audience. Do not collect unnecessary identifying information. A participant ID number (Participant 1, Participant 2, etc.) is sufficient.

---

## Ethical Considerations

- Participation is voluntary. Participants may withdraw at any time without explanation.
- No real names, contact information, or sensitive personal data should be collected or stored.
- Explain to participants that this is a student project to gather feedback, not a clinical study.
- Pre-test scores are baseline data, not evaluations of the participant's intelligence or ability.
- Do not share individual scores between participants.

## Pre/Post Score Comparison Method

1. Record each participant's pre-test score (0–6) in the results table.
2. Record each participant's post-test score (0–6) in the results table.
3. Calculate the difference (post minus pre) for each participant.
4. Calculate the average pre-test score and average post-test score across all participants.
5. Note which questions showed the greatest improvement and which did not.

**Interpretation guidance:**
- An improvement in average score suggests the lessons addressed knowledge gaps for this group.
- No improvement on a question suggests the concept may need clearer instruction.
- Do not use small-group data to claim general effectiveness. Report findings as preliminary observations.
- Note any ceiling effects: if a participant scored 6/6 on the pre-test, their post-test score cannot show improvement.

## Limitations

The following limitations apply to this pilot evaluation and must be acknowledged when reporting findings:

1. **Sample size:** A pilot of three to five volunteers is too small to draw conclusions about how the curriculum would perform with a larger or more diverse audience.
2. **Selection bias:** Volunteers who agree to participate in a curriculum pilot may be more motivated than the general target audience.
3. **Facilitator effect:** The person who created the curriculum may deliver it differently than an independent instructor. Pilot sessions run by the creator may not reflect typical delivery.
4. **Short-term measurement:** Pre/post testing within a single session measures immediate recall, not long-term retention.
5. **No control group:** This design does not include a comparison group, so changes in scores cannot be attributed solely to the instruction.
6. **Self-reported confidence:** The confidence check measures perceived understanding, not demonstrated understanding. High confidence does not confirm accuracy.
7. **Fictional scenario:** All pilot activities use fictional LunchRush scenarios. Findings may not predict how learners perform when applying concepts to other business contexts.

**Statement to include in any report:** "This pilot provides preliminary, qualitative feedback from a small convenience sample. The results are intended to identify revision opportunities, not to establish the effectiveness or generalizability of the curriculum."
